Math

Ratios and Proportional Relationships

Ratios and unit rates

A ratio compares two quantities. A unit rate tells how much for one unit: 150 miles in 3 hours is 50 miles per hour.

Try it: A car goes 240 miles in 4 hours. What is the unit rate?

Show answer

60 miles per hour.

Percent of a number

Percent means per 100. Change the percent to a decimal and multiply: 30% of 60 = 0.30 × 60 = 18.

Try it: What is 40% of 50?

Show answer

20.

Expressions and Equations

Evaluating expressions

Substitute a number for the variable, then follow the order of operations. If x = 4, then 3x + 2 = 3(4) + 2 = 14.

Try it: Evaluate 2n + 5 when n = 6.

Show answer

17.

One-step equations

Use inverse operations to get the variable alone. If x + 7 = 15, subtract 7 from both sides to get x = 8.

Try it: Solve 4n = 28.

Show answer

n = 7.

Geography

Latin America and the Caribbean

Major physical features

The Andes are the longest mountain range on land, running along the western edge of South America. The Amazon River and rain forest are in northern South America.

Try it: Which mountain range runs along western South America?

Show answer

The Andes.

Mexico and Brazil

Mexico's capital is Mexico City. Brazil is the largest country in South America, its capital is Brasília, and its official language is Portuguese.

Try it: What is the capital of Brazil?

Show answer

Brasília.

Europe

Landforms and waters

The Alps cross much of central Europe. The Danube River flows through several countries to the Black Sea, and the Mediterranean Sea lies to the south.

Try it: Which sea lies along southern Europe?

Show answer

The Mediterranean Sea.

The European Union

The European Union is a group of European countries that cooperate on trade and other issues. Many members use the euro as their currency.

Try it: What is the EU?

Show answer

A group of European countries that cooperate, especially on trade and the economy.

Language Arts

Reading Informational Text

Central idea and claims

The central idea is the main point of a text. An author's claim is what they want you to believe, supported by evidence.

Try it: What is the difference between a claim and evidence?

Show answer

A claim is the point the author argues. Evidence is the facts or examples that support it.

Evaluating arguments

A strong argument has a clear claim, relevant evidence, and sound reasoning. Watch for evidence that does not support the claim.

Try it: What makes evidence relevant?

Show answer

It directly supports the claim.

Writing

Argument writing

State a claim, support it with reasons and evidence, and address a counterclaim (an opposing view).

Try it: What is a counterclaim?

Show answer

An opposing viewpoint that the writer responds to.

Informative writing

Introduce the topic, organize ideas in groups, use transitions like first, next, and for example, and finish with a conclusion.

Try it: Give one transition word or phrase that introduces an example.

Show answer

For example (or for instance).
