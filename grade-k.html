Math

Counting and Cardinality

Counting to 100

Count by ones to 100, and count by tens: 10, 20, 30, and so on.

Try it: Count by tens. What number comes after 40?

Show answer

50.

Comparing numbers 1 to 10

The number that comes later when counting is greater. Use the words greater than, less than, and equal to.

Try it: Which is greater, 7 or 4?

Show answer

7 is greater than 4.

Operations and Algebraic Thinking

Adding means putting together

Adding combines groups. You can use objects, drawings, or fingers. 3 and 2 make 5.

Try it: What is 4 + 3?

Show answer

7.

Subtracting means taking away

Subtracting removes some from a group. Start with the whole group and count what is left.

Try it: There are 6 apples. You eat 2. How many are left?

Show answer

4 apples, because 6 − 2 = 4.

Geography

Maps and Globes

Globe and map

A globe is a round model of Earth. A map is a flat picture of a place.

Try it: Which one is round, a globe or a map?

Show answer

A globe.

Land and water

Earth has both land and water. Oceans cover most of Earth's surface.

Try it: Does water or land cover more of Earth?

Show answer

Water.

Map Skills

Map keys and symbols

A map key tells you what each symbol or color on the map means.

Try it: A map shows a blue wavy line. How can you find out what it stands for?

Show answer

Look at the map key.

Which way is north?

North is usually at the top of a map. South is at the bottom, east is on the right, and west is on the left.

Try it: If north is up, which way is east?

Show answer

To the right.

Language Arts

Phonics and Word Reading

Letter sounds and blending

Say each sound in a word, then blend them together: /c/ /a/ /t/ makes cat.

Try it: Blend these sounds: /d/ /o/ /g/.

Show answer

Dog.

Sight words

Some common words, like the, I, see, and like, show up so often that readers learn to know them on sight.

Try it: Which of these is a common sight word: the or zebra?

Show answer

The.

Reading Stories

Characters, setting, and events

Say who is in the story, where it happens, and the main things that happen.

Try it: In The Three Little Pigs, name one character.

Show answer

A pig, or the wolf.

Asking and answering questions

Use who, what, where, when, and why to think about a story.

Try it: Which question word asks about a place?

Show answer

Where.
