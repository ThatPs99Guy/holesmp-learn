Math

Operations and Algebraic Thinking

Multiplication as equal groups

Multiplication finds the total of equal groups. 4 groups of 6 is 4 × 6 = 24.

Try it: There are 3 bags with 5 marbles each. How many marbles in all?

Show answer

15, because 3 × 5 = 15.

Division as sharing

Division splits a total into equal groups. It is the opposite of multiplication, so 20 ÷ 4 = 5 because 4 × 5 = 20.

Try it: Share 18 cookies equally among 3 friends. How many does each get?

Show answer

6, because 18 ÷ 3 = 6.

Number and Operations: Fractions

Unit fractions

A unit fraction is one equal part of a whole, like 1/4. The bottom number tells how many equal parts the whole has.

Try it: A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices. You eat 3. What fraction did you eat?

Show answer

3/8.

Equivalent fractions

Different fractions can name the same amount. On a number line, 1/2 and 2/4 land on the same spot.

Try it: What fraction is equal to 3/6?

Show answer

1/2.

Geography

Map Skills and Location

Latitude and longitude

Latitude lines run east and west and measure distance from the equator. Longitude lines run north and south and measure distance from the prime meridian.

Try it: Do lines of latitude run east to west or north to south?

Show answer

East to west.

Hemispheres

The equator divides Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The prime meridian helps divide it into the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

Try it: Is the United States north or south of the equator?

Show answer

North.

Regions of the United States

Five regions

The U.S. is often grouped into five regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and West. Georgia is in the Southeast.

Try it: Which region is Georgia in?

Show answer

The Southeast.

Natural resources and land use

Natural resources like water, soil, and forests come from nature. People use land for farms, cities, and parks.

Try it: Is a forest a natural resource?

Show answer

Yes. Trees are a natural resource.

Language Arts

Reading Literature

Theme and central message

The theme is the lesson or big idea of a story. Think about what the character learns by the end.

Try it: A character learns that sharing helps make friends. What is the theme?

Show answer

Sharing helps build friendships.

Point of view

In first-person stories the narrator says I and tells their own experience. In third-person stories the narrator tells about he, she, or they.

Try it: Which point of view uses the word I to tell the story?

Show answer

First person.

Language and Vocabulary

Context clues

Look at the words around an unfamiliar word to figure out what it means.

Try it: The soup was scalding, so I waited for it to cool. What does scalding mean?

Show answer

Very hot.

Verb tenses

Tense shows when something happens: walk (now), walked (past), will walk (future). Some verbs change in special ways.

Try it: What is the past tense of run?

Show answer

Ran.
