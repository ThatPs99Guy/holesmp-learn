Math

Quadratic Functions

Solving by factoring

Factor, then set each factor to zero. x² − 5x + 6 = 0 gives (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so x = 2 or x = 3.

Try it: Solve x² − 7x + 12 = 0.

Show answer

x = 3 or x = 4.

The quadratic formula

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a. For x² + 2x − 3 = 0, the discriminant is 16, so x = 1 or x = −3.

Try it: Is the discriminant positive, zero, or negative when a quadratic has two real solutions?

Show answer

Positive.

Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Exponential growth

y = a(1 + r)ᵗ. $100 growing 5% a year for 2 years becomes 100 × 1.05² = $110.25.

Try it: $200 grows 10% a year for 2 years. What is the final amount?

Show answer

$242, because 200 × 1.1² = 242.

Geography

Geography and Westward Expansion

Manifest destiny and the West

In the 1800s, many Americans believed the nation should spread across the continent. Trails like the Oregon Trail carried settlers west.

Try it: What was the goal of travelers on the Oregon Trail?

Show answer

To reach the Oregon country in the Pacific Northwest.

Transportation and growth

The first transcontinental railroad was completed in 1869, cutting travel across the country from months to days.

Try it: In what year was the first transcontinental railroad completed?

Show answer

1869.

Migration in the United States

The Great Migration

Beginning around 1916, millions of African Americans moved from the rural South to cities in the North, Midwest, and West seeking jobs and fewer restrictions.

Try it: Name one reason people joined the Great Migration.

Show answer

Jobs, or escaping segregation and violence.

The Dust Bowl

In the 1930s, drought and poor farming practices turned Plains farmland into dust storms, pushing many families to leave, many toward California.

Try it: What caused the Dust Bowl?

Show answer

Drought combined with farming practices that left soil exposed.

Language Arts

American Literature

Literary movements

Romanticism stresses emotion and nature, Realism shows ordinary life, and Modernism breaks with tradition and experiments with form.

Try it: Which movement focuses on ordinary life as it really is?

Show answer

Realism.

Tone, mood, and irony

Tone is the author's attitude. Mood is the feeling readers get. Irony is a contrast between expectation and reality.

Try it: What is situational irony?

Show answer

When the outcome is the opposite of what is expected.

Writing

Persuasive argument

Strong arguments address counterclaims fairly and use rhetorical devices like parallelism, repetition, and strong evidence.

Try it: Name one rhetorical device.

Show answer

Repetition, parallelism, or a rhetorical question.

Analyzing historical texts

To analyze a speech or document, identify the author, audience, purpose, and the techniques used to persuade.

Try it: What questions help you analyze a speech?

Show answer

Who wrote it, who it is for, why, and how it persuades.
