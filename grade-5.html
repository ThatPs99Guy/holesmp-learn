Math

Number and Operations: Fractions

Adding and subtracting with unlike denominators

Rewrite the fractions with a common denominator, then add or subtract. 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Try it: What is 1/4 + 2/3?

Show answer

11/12, because 3/12 + 8/12 = 11/12.

Multiplying fractions

Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators, then simplify. 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5.

Try it: What is 3/4 × 2/5?

Show answer

6/20, which simplifies to 3/10.

Base Ten and Measurement

Rounding decimals

Look at the digit to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is 5 or more, round up. 3.46 rounds to 3.5 to the nearest tenth.

Try it: Round 6.72 to the nearest tenth.

Show answer

6.7.

Volume of rectangular prisms

Volume is the space inside a solid. Multiply length × width × height. A 4 by 3 by 2 prism has volume 24 cubic units.

Try it: Find the volume of a box 5 units long, 2 wide, and 3 tall.

Show answer

30 cubic units.

Geography

States and Capitals

Southeast capitals

Georgia: Atlanta. Alabama: Montgomery. Florida: Tallahassee. South Carolina: Columbia. North Carolina: Raleigh. Tennessee: Nashville.

Try it: What is the capital of Alabama?

Show answer

Montgomery.

Scale and distance

A map scale connects map distance to real distance. If 1 inch = 50 miles, then 3 inches = 150 miles.

Try it: If 1 inch = 20 miles, how far is 4 inches?

Show answer

80 miles.

Geography and U.S. History

How geography shaped movement

Mountains like the Appalachians slowed travel west. Rivers like the Mississippi worked as highways for people and goods.

Try it: Which was easier for moving goods in early America, a river or a mountain range?

Show answer

A river.

Reading population maps

Darker colors on a population map usually mean more people per area. Cities show up as dense spots.

Try it: On a population map, what do darker areas usually show?

Show answer

Places with more people.

Language Arts

Reading Literature

Quoting to support inferences

An inference is a conclusion based on clues. Use short quotes from the text to prove what you say.

Try it: Why do we use quotes from a text when explaining an inference?

Show answer

To show the evidence that supports our thinking.

Comparing stories

Compare and contrast two stories by looking at characters, settings, events, and themes. Say how they are alike and different.

Try it: Name two story parts you can compare between two books.

Show answer

Examples: characters and themes (or setting, or plot).

Language

Commas and conjunctions

Use commas in a list and after an introductory phrase: After lunch, we played outside. Conjunctions like and, but, and or join ideas.

Try it: Add a comma: After the game we went home.

Show answer

After the game, we went home.

Figurative language

A simile compares with like or as. A metaphor says one thing is another. An idiom has a meaning that is not literal.

Try it: Is He was a rock during the crisis a simile or a metaphor?

Show answer

A metaphor.
