1. Continents and oceans

The 7 continents

Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, and Australia (sometimes called Oceania). Asia is the largest by area and population. Australia is the smallest.

The 5 oceans

Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, and Arctic. The Pacific is the largest and deepest, covering more area than all the land on Earth combined.

Fun fact: The Sahara, the world's largest hot desert, is in northern Africa.

2. Latitude and longitude

Together, latitude and longitude give an address for any point on Earth.

LatitudeLongitude
Also calledParallelsMeridians
Lines runEast to west (parallel to the equator)North to south (pole to pole)
MeasuresDistance north or south of the equator (0°)Distance east or west of the prime meridian (0°)
Range0° to 90° N or S0° to 180° E or W

The equator splits Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The prime meridian, which passes through Greenwich, England, helps split it into the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

Example: 40° N, 74° W is near New York City: 40 degrees north of the equator and 74 degrees west of the prime meridian.

3. Reading a map

  • Compass rose: shows the directions north, south, east, and west.
  • Scale: tells you how a distance on the map matches real distance, such as 1 inch = 100 miles.
  • Legend (key): explains the symbols and colors used.
  • Title: tells you what the map is showing.
Example: If the scale is 1 inch = 50 miles and two cities are 3 inches apart on the map, they are about 3 × 50 = 150 miles apart.

Five regions of the United States

Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and West. Geographers sometimes group the states differently, so check which grouping your class uses.
