Math

Linear Equations and Inequalities

Solving linear inequalities

Solve like an equation, but flip the inequality sign when you multiply or divide by a negative. 2x − 3 > 7 gives x > 5.

Try it: Solve −2x < 8.

Show answer

x > −4, because dividing by −2 flips the sign.

Slope from two points

Slope = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). For (1, 2) and (3, 8), the slope is 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

Try it: Find the slope through (0, 1) and (4, 9).

Show answer

2.

Systems and Exponents

Solving systems by substitution

Substitute one equation into the other. For y = 2x and x + y = 9, x + 2x = 9, so x = 3 and y = 6.

Try it: Solve y = x + 1 and 2x + y = 7.

Show answer

x = 2, y = 3.

Exponent rules

Add exponents when multiplying powers with the same base, and multiply them for a power of a power: x³ · x⁴ = x⁷ and (x²)³ = x⁶.

Try it: Simplify x⁵ · x².

Show answer

x⁷.

Geography

Tools of Geography

The five themes

Geographers use five themes: location, place, human-environment interaction, movement, and region.

Try it: Which theme asks how people change their environment?

Show answer

Human-environment interaction.

Map projections

Flat maps distort Earth. The Mercator projection keeps directions accurate but stretches areas near the poles, making Greenland look huge.

Try it: What problem do all flat maps share?

Show answer

They distort size, shape, distance, or direction.

Population and Culture

Population density

Population density = people ÷ area. 1,000 people in 50 square miles is 20 people per square mile.

Try it: A town has 3,000 people in 10 square miles. What is its density?

Show answer

300 people per square mile.

Urbanization and migration

Urbanization is the growth of cities as people move from rural areas. Push and pull factors explain why people migrate.

Try it: Is moving for better schools a push or a pull factor?

Show answer

A pull factor.

Language Arts

Reading Literature

Theme, symbols, and character

Authors use symbols (objects that stand for ideas) and character change to develop a theme.

Try it: What is a symbol in literature?

Show answer

An object, person, or place that stands for a larger idea.

Structure and craft

A flashback shows earlier events, and foreshadowing hints at what will happen later.

Try it: Which technique gives hints about future events?

Show answer

Foreshadowing.

Writing

Thesis statements

A thesis states your main argument in one or two sentences and previews your reasons.

Try it: Is Dogs are animals a strong thesis?

Show answer

No. It states a fact, not an arguable claim.

Using and citing evidence

Introduce quotes, explain them, and give credit. In MLA style, a parenthetical citation gives the author and page, like (Smith 42).

Try it: Why do writers cite sources?

Show answer

To give credit, to let readers find the source, and to avoid plagiarism.
