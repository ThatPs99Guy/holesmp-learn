Math

Number and Operations: Fractions

Comparing fractions

To compare fractions, use common denominators or compare to a benchmark like 1/2. For 2/3 and 3/5: 10/15 and 9/15, so 2/3 is greater.

Try it: Which is greater, 3/4 or 5/8?

Show answer

3/4, because 6/8 is greater than 5/8.

Adding and subtracting with like denominators

Keep the denominator and add or subtract the numerators. 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8.

Try it: What is 7/10 − 3/10?

Show answer

4/10, which simplifies to 2/5.

Operations and Base Ten

Multiplying multi-digit numbers

Break the number apart. 23 × 4 = (20 × 4) + (3 × 4) = 80 + 12 = 92.

Try it: What is 31 × 5?

Show answer

155, because (30 × 5) + (1 × 5) = 150 + 5.

Factors and multiples

Factors multiply to make a number. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12. Multiples are what you get when you count by a number.

Try it: List the first three multiples of 7.

Show answer

7, 14, 21.

Geography

Physical Geography of the United States

Major landforms

The Appalachian Mountains are in the east and the Rocky Mountains are in the west. Between them lie large plains.

Try it: Which mountains are in the eastern United States?

Show answer

The Appalachian Mountains.

Rivers and lakes

The Mississippi River flows south to the Gulf of Mexico. The five Great Lakes are Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, and Ontario.

Try it: Name one of the Great Lakes.

Show answer

Any of Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, or Ontario.

People and Places

Why people settle where they do

People often settle near water, fertile soil, and transportation routes. Rivers helped with travel, drinking water, and farming.

Try it: Why might people settle near a river?

Show answer

For water, farming, and travel.

Moving to a new place

Push factors make people leave a place, such as a lack of jobs. Pull factors draw them to another place, such as good schools.

Try it: Is a job opening in a new city a push or a pull factor?

Show answer

A pull factor.

Language Arts

Reading Informational Text

Main idea and summary

The main idea is what the text is mostly about. A summary uses your own words to give the main idea and the most important details.

Try it: What should a summary include?

Show answer

The main idea and key details, in your own words.

Firsthand and secondhand accounts

A firsthand account is written by someone who was there. A secondhand account is told by someone who learned about it from others.

Try it: Is a diary entry about a storm a firsthand or secondhand account?

Show answer

Firsthand, if the writer lived through the storm.

Writing

Opinion writing

State your opinion, give reasons, and use linking words like because and for example. End with a concluding sentence.

Try it: Write one reason for this opinion: Our class should have a garden.

Show answer

Sample answer: A garden teaches students how plants grow.

Dialogue in narratives

Use quotation marks around a speaker's exact words, and start a new paragraph when a new person speaks.

Try it: Add quotation marks to the spoken words: Come here, said Mom.

Show answer

“Come here,” said Mom.
